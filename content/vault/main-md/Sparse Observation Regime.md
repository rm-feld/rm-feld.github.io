> [!attention] Sparse Observation Regime (Binary $Z$)
> Following the motivation of the [[Netflix Problem Setting|Netflix Problem]], Consider two high cardinality categorical variables, indexed by $i$ and $j$ respectively, and let $Z_{ij} = 1$ if there exists an observation which is indexed by both $i$ and $j$. 
> %% %%
> We are interested in the regime where unique $i$ and unique $j$ are large. For $N$ observations, we formalize this by setting the number of unique indices $I = \Omega(N^{\kappa_{1}})$, $J = \Omega(N^{\kappa_{2}})$ such that $\kappa_{1} + \kappa_{2} > 1$. In this way the proportion of unique crosses observed is upper bounded by $N^{1 - \kappa_{1} - \kappa_{2}}\to 0$ as $N \to \infty$.
